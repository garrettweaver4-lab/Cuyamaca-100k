What each leg actually asks of you

Start → Merrigan

8.4 mi  ▲380  ▼1,060

Rolling singletrack that gives back 680 ft. It is 52°F and dim — sunrise is 6:45, so the first 15 minutes want a light. This split is the one people blow. If it feels easy you are probably still 30–45 sec/mi too fast; the day is decided three hours from now.

Merrigan → Green Valley

5.8 mi  ▲900  ▼320

The course turns and starts taking back what it gave. Runnable grade along the Cuyamaca Reservoir Historic Trail. Leave Green Valley with both bottles full and salt in you — the next nine miles are the hardest of the race and there is no aid in them.

Green Valley → Cuyamaca Peak

9.0 mi  ▲2,900  ▼370

Dips to Arroyo Seco first, then climbs 2,900 ft to 6,512. The last stretch is paved Lookout Fire Road, fully exposed, and by now it is mid-morning and warming fast. Hike the steep pitches on purpose rather than by collapse. Budget 2:00–2:50 here and do not panic at the pace number.

Cuyamaca Peak → Paso Picacho

5.0 mi  ▲180  ▼1,830

Free speed, and the single easiest place on the course to wreck your quads. Descend controlled — you still have a 5,000 ft climb-and-descent day ahead of you. Take ice here if it is offered.

Paso Picacho → Camp Cuyamaca

3.6 mi  ▲180  ▼960

Downhill to the end of Loop 1 and your first drop bag. Loop 1 is roughly 45–46% of your total time. If you are much under that, you went out too hard.

Camp Cuyamaca → East Mesa

8.0 mi  ▲1,250  ▼430

The hot one. Steep Dyar Springs ascent straight out of the aid station, then exposed chaparral and meadow across East Mesa, and you hit it between 1 and 4 PM. Leave camp with ice in a bandana, full capacity, and no ego. Most DNFs start being written here.

East Mesa → Camp Cuyamaca

4.8 mi  ▲200  ▼1,020

Net downhill back to camp. Still hot, but gravity is on your side. This is a free-money segment — if you have kept your core temperature down you can claw back five minutes here.

Camp Cuyamaca → Sunrise

6.8 mi  ▲1,700  ▼300

Longest sustained climb of the back half, 1,700 ft up toward the PCT. Pick up your headlamp at camp whatever your goal — sunset is 6:28 and full dark by 6:55. Grab your pacer here if you have one.

Sunrise → Pedro Fages

4.6 mi  ▲550  ▼800

PCT above the Anza-Borrego desert. Rolling, spectacular at dusk, and rockier than it looks — runners consistently stub toes through here in the dark. Shorten your stride, raise your feet, and accept 60–90 sec/mi slower than the profile suggests.

Pedro Fages → Finish

6.8 mi  ▲450  ▼1,600

Net 1,150 ft down through the northern meadows and back to camp. Genuinely runnable if anything is left. Under 17 hours here makes it a Western States qualifier; the hard cutoff is 19.

How these numbers were built

A flat pace chart would tell you to run 10:31/mi to break eleven hours, which is useless on a course that hands you a 2,900 ft climb in the middle of loop one. Instead each of the ten legs got a difficulty index, and the goal time was distributed across those indices.

  • Terrain and grade. The climb to Cuyamaca Peak is weighted at roughly 1.4× average course effort per mile; the descent off it at about 0.83×. The opening eight miles to Merrigan, downhill and fresh, sit near 0.80×.
  • Time in race. A fade factor grows with distance so later legs run slower relative to early ones — about 10% of drift for an 11-hour plan, 24% for a 16-hour plan. Faster runners fade less, which is what the results data shows.
  • Heat of day. Camp → East Mesa is the exposed chaparral crossing and almost everyone hits it between 1 and 4 PM. It carries a penalty above what its 1,250 ft of gain alone would justify. The Peak climb gets a smaller one for the same reason.
  • Darkness and footing. The rocky PCT stretch from Sunrise to Pedro Fages is weighted for headlamp running, not for its modest vert.
  • Aid station time. Budgeted separately and shown as a departure time, not folded into pace. Eleven-hour plans get 11 minutes total across nine stops; sixteen-hour plans get 46, weighted toward the two drop-bag stops at camp.

Treat the arrival clock as the number that matters and the pace column as explanation. Being two minutes off at Merrigan means nothing. Being twenty minutes off at the end of loop one means you should quietly move to the next goal column down.
